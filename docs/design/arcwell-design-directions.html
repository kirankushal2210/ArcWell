Arcwell · Design directions · for review

Three ways Arcwell
could feel like Apple made it.

Each direction follows Apple's current Human Interface Guidelines and borrows from one family of Apple's own apps. Same screens, same data, three treatments, so you can compare them side by side.

What Apple's own apps have in common

Content first. Glass for controls. Colour with a job.

These come from Apple's guidelines for colour and materials and from Health, Fitness, Settings and the iOS 26 apps. Every direction below keeps them.

1

Glass is for controls, never content

Tab bars, toolbars and floating buttons sit on glass, so content scrolls and shows through. Cards and backgrounds stay solid. Glass is never stacked on glass.

2

One coloured button per screen

Apple puts the accent on the background of the single most important action, like Done. Everything else is grey, tinted or plain text.

3

Colour labels categories

In Health, each category owns a colour (activity, nutrition, sleep) on a small rounded square and its title. Colour tells you where you are; it is not decoration.

4

Big titles, big numbers

Large titles name the screen. Numbers are bold and tabular, with small units beside them. Hierarchy comes from weight and size, not boxes and borders.

5

Grouped lists for structure

Grey background, white inset groups, hairline separators, a chevron where a row opens something. Instantly readable, and coaches scan it fast.

6

No ornament

No gradients, glows or decorative blobs. Depth comes from materials, spacing and a soft shadow under floating controls only.

Shared by every direction

The foundations

The pillar colours are Apple's own system colours, as Health uses them: orange for activity, green for nutrition, indigo for sleep. Light mode uses Apple's increased-contrast shades, so white text on them passes AA.

Coach Clients. Search, a segmented control and an inset list, like Mail or Reminders. Each row offers one action.

Best for

Coaches at a desk or between sessions, data-heavy screens, building trust with first-time users.

Trade-off

The calmest of the three. It may feel less energetic to a 25-year-old gym-goer than B.

Build effort

Lowest. Very close to the current structure; mostly new colours, type and tab bar.

Direction B

Fitness. Black, bold, numbers first.

Learns from Fitness and the Workout app: true black, rings, and very large bold numbers in each pillar's colour. Energetic and athletic, and easy on the eyes in a dim gym.

Direction C

Liquid Glass. Content edge to edge.

Learns from the iOS 26 apps (Music, Photos, Weather): content runs to the edges, and controls float above it on glass: round toolbar buttons, a floating tab bar, sheets that rise from the bottom. The most current and the most "Apple" of the three.

Coach Clients. Search floats at the bottom where the thumb is, as in iOS 26. The list scrolls under the glass controls.

Best for

The most modern, premium first impression. Strong on screens with one clear focus: Today, logging, check-in review.

Trade-off

Arcwell is an Android app. True Liquid Glass (lensing, live refraction) exists only on iOS 26. On Android we can match the frosted look with blur, but not the bending of light, and heavy blur costs battery on cheaper phones.

Build effort

Highest. New floating chrome, sheets and per-screen layouts.

My recommendation

Glass chrome over Health structure, with Fitness for workouts.

This mix is how Apple's own apps already behave: Health is light and grouped, the Workout app is black, and both sit under iOS 26 glass controls.

  • Everywhere: C's floating glass tab bar, round toolbar buttons and bottom sheets.
  • Content: A's grouped cards and category colours for Today, Nutrition, Recovery and every coach screen.
  • During a workout: B's black screen with huge numbers, because people read it at arm's length mid-set.
  • Dark mode: follows the phone's setting, using the dark values of the same colours.

Two notes. The mockups use Inter: Apple's San Francisco font is licensed only for Apple platforms, so it can't ship in an Android app, and Inter is the closest free match. The rings here use our own pillar colours rather than Apple's red, green and blue Activity rings, which Apple asks apps not to imitate.
