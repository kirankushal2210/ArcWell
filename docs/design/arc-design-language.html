Design language proposal · v2 · for founder review

Arc: the colour of
a day well lived.

Arcwell moves from black and white to a dark, warm, alive look built on one idea: your day has an arc. You fuel up in the morning (saffron), push through effort (flame) and recover at night (dusk). Each pillar keeps one colour everywhere, and together they make the Arcwell gradient: training, nutrition and recovery, coached as one.

The goal is an app people want to open, because opening it shows them their real progress. Not because it tricks them into staying.

01 · The concept

Three pillars, one arc

Colour stops being decoration and becomes navigation. Whatever pillar you're in, its colour fills the main button, the rings and the highlights, so you always know where you are without reading a label.

Saffron · Nutrition

03 · Colour system

Dark first, with a warm light theme

Dark suits gyms, evenings and the OLED Android phones most of your users have, and it makes the three pillar colours glow. Neutrals lean slightly violet so they belong to the dusk end of the arc instead of looking like default grey. Text on bright buttons is dark ink, which keeps every pair above the WCAG AA contrast level.

Rules for using colour

  • The pillar you're in owns the primary button. Start workout is flame, Log meal is saffron, Start wind-down is dusk. Screens that belong to no pillar (onboarding, coach tools, paywall) use the Arc gradient.
  • One primary per screen. Everything else is tonal (raised ink) or a text button. Colour means "do this now".
  • The gradient is earned. It appears on the logo, the centre + button, unseen coach stories, celebrations and the coach's queue. Never as a page background.
  • Status never relies on colour alone (C11 stays): every status tag has a dot and a word, every alert an icon.
  • No shaming red. Eating over target, a missed workout or a low recovery score are amber or plain text, never rose. Rose is only for errors and destructive actions.

04 · Type

Inter, but with a voice

We keep Inter (D46), but stop setting everything at polite sizes. Big numbers are the hero of a fitness app, so they get a heavy weight, tight tracking and tabular figures. Large text tightens, small text opens up.

06 · Motion and feel

Things move like they have weight

All motion uses springs (Reanimated), so anything can be grabbed and reversed mid-way. Default springs settle without bounce. Only moves that follow a flick, or a real achievement, get a little overshoot.

Rings fill, numbers roll

Tap the ring. When a set is logged or a meal is eaten, the ring springs forward and the number counts up, with a light haptic in the same frame.

Where motion goes

  • Press: squash to 96% on press-in, light haptic. 120 ms.
  • Set done: tick fills, row tints flame, success haptic.
  • New best: saffron outline, star burst, heavy haptic. Once per exercise.
  • Workout finished: the one full-screen moment. Rings close, volume rolls up, bests slide in, share card ready.
  • Sheets: rise from the button that opened them, follow your finger, flick to dismiss.
  • Reduced motion on: everything cross-fades instead; haptics stay.

07 · Why people come back

The habit loop, done honestly

Instagram and Netflix keep people with a clear loop: a trigger, a fast action, a reward and something you've built. Arcwell uses the same loop, but every reward is real progress you made. This is the part that makes an app feel hard to put down.

Trigger

A reason to open

Your coach replied. Your recovery score is ready. It's check-in day. One useful notification at a time, never "we miss you".

Action

One tap to the thing

The notification opens the exact screen. The centre + logs a set, meal, weight or sleep in two taps.

Reward

Visible progress

A ring closes. A new best. The trend line dips. Your coach says "great week". Rewards you can't fake.

Investment

Something you've built

Weeks of consistency, a photo timeline, milestones, a shareable progress card for Instagram.

Weekly consistency, not daily streaks

Hit your planned sessions for the week and the week turns gold. Rest days count as kept. Miss one and the week is still yours to save. No streak resets at midnight.

Milestones

Earned, private and quiet: first 10 workouts, 100 kg total lifted in a session, 4 check-ins in a row, a 5 kg trend change. Shown on Progress; shareable if you choose.

Share cards

After a workout, a best or a check-in, one tap makes a story-sized card in the Arc gradient. Athletes show off; coaches get free marketing on Instagram, where their leads come from.

08 · Guardrails

Pull, never trap

This is a health app, used by people working on their bodies, sometimes during hard times. These rules sit above any engagement goal.

✕

No endless feeds. Every screen has an end. There's nothing to scroll for its own sake.

✕

No guilt or fake urgency. No "you'll lose your streak", no countdowns, no "only 2 spots left" in the athlete app.

✕

No shaming food or weight. No red for eating over target, no "bad" days, no body comparisons with other people.

✓

Quiet hours by default (10 pm to 7 am), and at most one non-coach notification a day.

✓

Photos and shares are opt-in, every time. Share cards never include body photos unless the athlete picks them.

✓

Coached athletes still never see AI. The new + menu and Today keep that rule.

09 · What changes where

Screen by screen

Athlete

  • Tab bar: Today · Train · + · Fuel · Recover. The + opens a quick-log sheet.
  • Today: pillar story rings, coach story, billboard for the next action, week strip, meals shelf, tonight card.
  • Training: program as poster cards; logging in full-screen flame mode with swipe-to-complete sets and a live rest island.
  • Nutrition: saffron day ring, meal cards with one-tap "Ate this", Indian portions as chips.
  • Recovery: dusk mode, calmer motion, rings with a soft glow, wind-down as one button.
  • Progress: drag-to-compare photos, milestones shelf, share cards.
  • Check-in: a guided, one-question-per-screen flow that ends in a "sent" moment.

Coach

  • Clients: "Today's queue" hero with one action per client; avatars become triple pillar rings.
  • Check-ins: review as a flow: compare, reply, "Send and next" until the queue is clear.
  • Plans: templates as covers in pillar colours; builders keep the shared editors.
  • Messages: your bubbles in the Arc gradient, theirs in ink; quick replies as chips.
  • Business: big rolling numbers with sparklines; funnel bars in the gradient; Instagram tab gets its own warm accent.
  • Menu: a sheet that rises from the hamburger, not a separate page.

10 · Plan

How we roll it out

The app already reads every colour, size and radius from one theme folder, so changing the tokens restyles most screens at once. Then the components, then each screen in turn.

1

Foundations

Record the decision (replacing D50 monochrome, "no gradients, glows or badges" and the glass ban for the tab bar). New tokens in DESIGN.md, theme-tokens.css and app/src/theme: dark palette, pillar colours, type scale, radii, spring presets, haptics. Dark ships first; the light theme follows once screens read colours through a theme hook.

2

Components

  • Button (pillar pills, tonal, ghost, danger), IconButton, Chip, Tag, Card, ListRow and Group
  • Ring and bars with spring fill, number roll, story ring, triple ring avatar
  • Floating translucent tab bar with the centre +, bottom sheet, toast, celebration, share card
3

Athlete screens

Today first (it's opened most), then workout logging and the summary, Nutrition, Recovery, Progress, the weekly check-in, chat.

4

Coach screens

Clients and the queue, the check-in review flow, Plans, Messages, Business, the menu sheet.

5

Onboarding, welcome and polish

Welcome, sign-in and both onboarding flows; then a pass on contrast, 130% text, reduced motion and real-phone testing in Expo Go.

11 · Your call

Four decisions before we build

Dark first?

You moved the app to light, then black and white, earlier. Arc works best dark, with a light theme to follow.

Recommended: dark first, light theme in Phase 5.

The Arc palette: saffron, flame and dusk?

It replaces the current small cyan, amber and violet dots. Alternatives would be a cooler set (electric blue, lime, violet) or a premium set (indigo and gold).

Recommended: Arc, for its Indian warmth and the "day" story.

Centre + on the athlete tab bar?

Fastest logging, but it moves the Pro AI button from a floating bubble into the + sheet and the Today header.

Recommended: yes.

Weekly consistency, milestones and share cards?

This changes the brand line "not gamified, no streaks" to "earned, honest rewards", within the guardrails above.

Recommended: yes, all three.
