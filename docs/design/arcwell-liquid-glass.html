Colour

No grey, no cream. Five vivid colours on black.

Why this holds attention: on a black OLED screen, saturated colour is the only light there is, so the eye goes straight to it. Each colour has one job, so after a day people read the app by colour before they read a word.

Colour rules

  • Colour on black, black on colour. Coloured numbers on dark backgrounds; black labels on coloured buttons. White text never sits on a vivid colour (it fails contrast).
  • One coloured action per screen, in the colour of the pillar you're in. Everything else is glass or plain.
  • Coral only means "new" or "needs you". Unread dots, overdue check-ins, destructive buttons. Never for eating over a target.
  • Glass stays clear. Controls are frosted glass that picks up the colour scrolling under it; the controls themselves are white, with one tinted primary at most.

Layout

Built around the thumb, so the brain never searches.

Most people hold the phone in one hand. The bottom third of the screen is where the thumb rests, so everything you tap often lives there, always in the same spot. The top is for reading.

Read hereTitles, numbers, coach note.
Rare controls only (profile, menu).
Scan and tap hereCards, rows, toggles.
Act hereThe one action, tab bar, the + button.
Same place on every screen.
1

Three fixed spots

Action dock (the screen's one main action, full width, just above the tab bar), tab bar (bottom left) and the + button (bottom right). They never move, so after a week the thumb goes there without looking.

2

The next step replaces the last one

Tap Log set 2 and the button becomes Log set 3, in the same place. Send a check-in reply and it becomes Next client. You never hunt for what to do next.

3

Fewer choices, bigger targets

One main action and at most two others per screen. The main action is 56 dp tall and full width; everything tappable is at least 48 dp, with 8 dp between targets. Big and close is fast to hit.

4

Swipe instead of reach

Swipe left and right between section tabs. Swipe a set or meal row right to complete it. Drag a sheet down to close it. Gestures happen where the thumb already is.

5

Shortcuts everywhere

Long-press the app icon: Start workout, Log meal, Message coach. Long-press a tab for its top action. Notifications carry buttons: reply to your coach or log water without opening the app.

Feel

No waiting, anywhere.

Speed is what makes people come back without thinking. These are the targets the build will be checked against.

0 ms

Respond on touch-down

Buttons react the moment the finger lands, not when it lifts: a quick squash and a light haptic.

2 taps

To log anything

+ then Workout, Meal, Weight, Photo or Water. A set is one tap. A planned meal is one tap.

No

"Are you sure?"

Actions happen instantly with Undo for five seconds. Only truly permanent things (delete account) ask first.

0 spinners

Content is already there

Screens show saved data at once and update quietly. Every log appears before the server confirms it.

What I'll build

  1. Foundations: the five colours and the tab colour fields as tokens, dark-only to start, Inter at Apple's text sizes, spring timings, haptics. Recorded as a decision replacing the black-and-white look (D50).
  2. The glass layer: the floating tab bar, the + button and sheet, round glass toolbar buttons and the action dock, as shared components with blur on Android.
  3. Athlete screens: Today, logging, Nutrition, Recovery, the check-in and chat, each with its dock action and gestures.
  4. Coach screens: Clients, the review run, Plans, Messages, Business.
  5. Speed pass: respond on press, undo instead of confirm, saved data shown instantly, app-icon shortcuts.

On Android, glass is frosted blur: it can't bend light the way iOS 26 does. On low-end phones we'll fall back to a solid dark glass colour, so the app stays fast.
