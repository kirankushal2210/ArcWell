Arcwell wordmark directions

Three directions for the founder to choose from (handoff step 2). Every one uses Inter (D46), solid colours only (D24: no gradients, no glows) and the spec-sheet tokens. Each is shown large, at app-bar size, as an app icon, in one colour for photo exports (D45), and on light.

The type is set in Inter here. The chosen direction would then be drawn as outlined paths with its spacing tuned by hand.

A · Ring

A three-quarter ring next to a lowercase wordmark. The ring is the progress ring from the workout summary (the one 600 ms "big moment"), so the logo and the app's best moment share one shape. The open quarter means "still in progress", which fits consistency over intensity. The ring alone becomes the app icon.

B · Arch

An "A" monogram drawn as one arch, with a short cyan horizon line as the crossbar, next to a wide-spaced capital wordmark. An arch is the strongest load-bearing shape, so it says "built properly, built to last": the serious-coach side of the brand. It looks the most architectural and premium of the three, and the least like a fitness app.

C · Open c

No separate symbol. The "c" in the name is redrawn as an open cyan arc, so the arc lives inside the word itself. It's the quietest and most typographic option, close to how Linear and Oura sign themselves. For the app icon, the arc sits alone on charcoal. Risk: at small sizes it reads as an ordinary "c", which is fine but less distinctive.

How they compare

A · Ring

Most "app-like" and the easiest to recognise as an icon. Ties straight into the product's ring and dial visuals (workout summary, sleep score). Risk: rings are common in fitness and health apps.

B · Arch

Most distinctive, and the strongest fit for "serious coach's tool". Uses both brand colours. Risk: the caps wordmark is formal, and the monogram needs care so it doesn't read as an "n".

C · Open c

Most restrained and the most "premium software". One mark everywhere, no separate symbol. Risk: weakest as a stand-alone app icon.
